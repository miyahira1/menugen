# Agedashi Tofu

> Silken tofu, lightly dusted in potato starch and deep-fried until golden, served in a warm delicate dashi broth. One of the most elegant vegetarian dishes in Japanese cuisine.

## Ingredients

- 600 g (1.3 lbs) medium-firm tofu (momen or cotton tofu)
- 80 g (½ cup) potato starch or cornstarch
- Neutral oil, for frying

**Tentsuyu broth:**
- 300 ml (1¼ cups) dashi stock
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 1 tsp sugar

**Garnishes:**
- 5 cm daikon radish, finely grated (daikon oroshi)
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
- 2 spring onions, finely sliced
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
- Bonito flakes (katsuobushi), optional

## Instructions

1. **Press tofu:** Wrap tofu in paper towels, place on a plate with a weight on top (e.g. a chopping board). Rest 20 minutes to drain excess moisture — this is essential for good browning and prevents oil spitting.
2. **Make broth:** Combine dashi, soy sauce, mirin, and sugar in a small saucepan. Bring to a simmer and cook for 3 minutes. Taste — it should be light, savoury, and slightly sweet. Keep warm.
3. **Prepare tofu:** Cut drained tofu into 6–8 rectangular cubes, about 5 × 4 × 3 cm. Pat completely dry with fresh paper towels.
4. **Coat:** Roll each piece gently in potato starch, tapping off all excess. The coating should be a thin, even veil.
5. **Fry:** Heat oil to 180 °C / 355 °F. Fry tofu pieces in batches for 3–4 minutes, turning gently, until pale golden and the crust is set. Drain on a wire rack.
6. **Serve:** Place 2 pieces of tofu in each serving bowl. Pour warm broth around the tofu (not over it). Top with grated daikon, ginger, spring onion, and sesame seeds.

## Chef's Tips

- Pressing the tofu is non-negotiable — wet tofu splatters violently and won't crisp.
- Medium-firm tofu holds its shape during frying while staying creamy inside; silken tofu will fall apart.
- The broth is poured around not over the tofu to preserve the crispy exterior as long as possible.
- Eat immediately — the crust softens as it sits in the broth (though that is also delicious in its own way).

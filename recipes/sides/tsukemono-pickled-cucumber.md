# Tsukemono Pickled Cucumber

> Crisp, lightly salted Japanese pickled cucumbers — essential at every Japanese meal as a palate-cleanser, and ready in just 30 minutes of resting.

## Ingredients

- 4 Japanese or Persian cucumbers (about 500 g / 1.1 lbs) — do not use thick European cucumbers
- 1½ tsp fine sea salt
- 2 tsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
- ½ tsp dried chilli flakes, optional
- 2 cm piece fresh ginger, cut into thin matchsticks

## Instructions

1. **Prepare cucumbers:** Wash cucumbers. Score lightly all over with a fork for texture (optional). Slice into thin rounds or diagonal slices about 4 mm thick. Alternatively, smash with the flat side of a knife, then tear into bite-sized irregular pieces — this is the shiozuke (smashed) style that absorbs flavour faster.
2. **Salt:** Toss cucumber slices with fine salt in a bowl. Mix well and leave for 15–20 minutes. The salt will draw out excess water.
3. **Squeeze:** Drain the liquid and squeeze the cucumbers firmly with your hands to remove as much moisture as possible. They should feel limp.
4. **Season:** Add rice vinegar, sugar, soy sauce, sesame oil, sesame seeds, ginger, and chilli flakes (if using). Toss to coat.
5. **Rest:** Leave for 10 more minutes for flavours to meld. Taste and adjust — more salt, more vinegar, or a pinch more sugar.
6. **Serve** at room temperature or chilled. Best eaten the same day.

## Chef's Tips

- Japanese cucumbers are thinner skinned and less watery than European cucumbers — the difference is significant.
- Smashing the cucumbers (rather than slicing) creates crevices that absorb the dressing far more effectively.
- This is easily adapted: swap ginger for garlic, add yuzu zest, or use a splash of ponzu instead of soy + rice vinegar.
- For a Korean-adjacent variation, add 1 tsp gochujang and 1 tsp fish sauce.

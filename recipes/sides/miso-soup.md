# Miso Soup

> The cornerstone of Japanese cooking — a delicate, umami-rich broth with tofu and wakame that comes together in under 15 minutes.

## Ingredients

- 1 litre (4 cups) dashi stock (or water + 1 tbsp dashi powder)
- 4 tbsp white (shiro) miso paste
- 150 g (5 oz) soft tofu, cut into 1.5 cm cubes
- 10 g (0.3 oz) dried wakame seaweed
- 2 spring onions (scallions), thinly sliced
- Optional: 4 small mushrooms (shiitake or enoki)

## Instructions

1. **Rehydrate wakame:** Soak dried wakame in cold water for 5 minutes. Drain, squeeze out excess water, and set aside.
2. **Heat dashi:** Bring dashi to a gentle simmer over medium heat. Do not boil — keep it just below a simmer throughout.
3. **Add tofu and mushrooms:** Gently add tofu cubes (and mushrooms if using). Simmer for 2 minutes.
4. **Dissolve miso:** Reduce heat to low. Put miso paste into a small strainer or ladle, lower it into the broth, and stir with chopsticks until dissolved. Never boil miso — it kills the probiotics and dulls the flavour.
5. **Add wakame:** Stir in the rehydrated wakame.
6. **Serve:** Ladle into bowls and top with spring onions. Serve immediately.

## Chef's Tips

- White miso (shiro) is mild and sweet — use red miso (aka) for a deeper, saltier flavour.
- A proper dashi from kombu and katsuobushi is worth making in batches and freezing.
- Add a drizzle of toasted sesame oil for extra depth.
- In Japan, miso soup is sipped directly from the bowl — no spoon needed.

# Chawanmushi

> Japan's silkiest steamed egg custard — delicate, savoury, and set with dashi instead of cream. Filled with chicken, shrimp, and mushroom, it wobbles like jelly and melts on the tongue.

## Ingredients

**Custard base:**
- 4 large eggs
- 600 ml (2½ cups) dashi stock, cooled
- 2 tsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp mirin
- ½ tsp fine salt

**Fillings:**
- 100 g (3.5 oz) boneless chicken thigh, cut into small cubes
- 8 medium raw shrimp (prawns), peeled and deveined
- 4 shiitake mushrooms, stems removed, caps quartered
- 8 slices kamaboko (fish cake), optional
- 4 small sprigs mitsuba or flat-leaf parsley

**Marinade for chicken:**
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp sake

## Instructions

1. **Marinate chicken:** Toss chicken with soy sauce and sake. Rest 5 minutes.
2. **Make custard:** Beat eggs gently — do not incorporate air (no frothiness). Stir in cooled dashi, soy sauce, mirin, and salt. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve twice for an ultra-smooth custard. This step is what gives chawanmushi its silky texture.
3. **Prep cups:** Divide chicken, shrimp, and mushrooms among 4 chawanmushi cups or ramekins (200 ml / 7 oz capacity).
4. **Fill:** Slowly pour custard over the fillings through the sieve. Fill to about 1 cm from the top. Remove any surface bubbles with a paper towel.
5. **Steam:** Cover each cup tightly with foil (or the lid if using chawanmushi cups). Place in a steamer basket over gently simmering water. Steam on medium-low heat (not a rolling boil) for 12–14 minutes until custard is just set — it should wobble slightly in the centre. A skewer inserted should come out clean.
6. **Garnish:** Remove foil and place a mitsuba sprig on top. Serve immediately in the cups with spoons.

## Chef's Tips

- The dashi must be cool (not hot) before mixing with eggs — hot dashi pre-cooks the eggs and creates lumps.
- Low, steady steam is everything — high heat creates bubbles and a pitted, rough texture instead of smooth custard.
- Cover the cups with foil even if they have lids — it prevents condensation droplets from dripping onto the custard.
- For a luxurious version, add a spoonful of ikura (salmon roe) or a prawn head for presentation just before serving.

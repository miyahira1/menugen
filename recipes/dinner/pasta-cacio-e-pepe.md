# Cacio e Pepe

> Rome's most deceptively simple pasta — just three ingredients, but technique is everything. Silky, peppery, intensely cheesy.

## Ingredients

- 400 g (14 oz) tonnarelli or spaghetti
- 200 g (7 oz) Pecorino Romano, finely grated (use a microplane)
- 50 g (1.75 oz) Parmigiano Reggiano, finely grated
- 2 tsp whole black peppercorns, coarsely cracked
- Fine sea salt (for pasta water)
- 2–3 tbsp pasta cooking water (key!)

## Instructions

1. **Toast the pepper:** Toast cracked pepper in a large dry skillet over medium heat for 1 minute until fragrant. Remove from heat and set aside.
2. **Cook pasta:** Bring a large pot of water to boil. Use less water than usual (about 3 litres) so the starch concentration is higher. Salt lightly — Pecorino is salty. Cook pasta 2 minutes less than the packet says.
3. **Make cheese paste:** In a bowl, mix both grated cheeses together. Add 3–4 tbsp of the pasta cooking water to form a thick, lump-free paste. The water must not be boiling — let it cool 30 seconds first.
4. **Emulsify:** Reserve 250 ml (1 cup) of pasta water before draining. Return the skillet with pepper to low heat. Add 60 ml (¼ cup) pasta water and let it reduce slightly.
5. **Finish pasta:** Add the drained pasta to the skillet and toss over low heat. Remove pan from heat entirely. Add cheese paste in two additions, tossing vigorously and adding pasta water a splash at a time to achieve a creamy, coating sauce. The off-heat step is critical — too much heat scrambles the cheese.
6. **Serve immediately** in warm bowls. Finish with extra Pecorino and cracked pepper.

## Chef's Tips

- The pasta water starch is the emulsifier — don't skip it.
- Use a microplane for the cheese: coarse grating creates lumps.
- Work quickly once you add the cheese. If the sauce seizes, a splash of warm pasta water and vigorous tossing will rescue it.
- Tonnarelli (square spaghetti) is traditional and grips the sauce better.

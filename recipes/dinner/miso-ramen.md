# Miso Ramen

> Sapporo's signature bowl — a rich, warming miso-based broth with chewy ramen noodles, chashu pork, soft egg, and corn. A simplified but deeply satisfying home version.

## Ingredients

**Broth:**
- 1.2 litres (5 cups) chicken or pork stock (good quality)
- 4 tbsp white miso paste
- 2 tbsp red miso paste
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp mirin
- 1 tbsp sake
- 1 tbsp sesame paste (tahini or white sesame paste)
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 1 tsp chilli paste (toban djan or gochujang)

**Noodles:**
- 400 g (14 oz) fresh or dried ramen noodles (or substitute spaghetti)

**Toppings:**
- 4 soft-boiled eggs (ramen eggs: marinated 30 mins in 2 tbsp soy + 1 tbsp mirin after peeling)
- 200 g (7 oz) thinly sliced pork belly or char siu pork
- Tinned corn kernels (100 g)
- 2 spring onions, sliced
- Bamboo shoots (menma), optional
- 1 tbsp butter per bowl
- Bean sprouts, briefly blanched

## Instructions

1. **Marinate eggs (optional but recommended):** Boil eggs for 6 minutes 30 seconds, cool in ice water, peel, and soak in a mix of 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp mirin, and water to cover for at least 30 minutes (up to 24 hours).
2. **Make the tare:** In a small saucepan, warm 1 tbsp sesame oil over medium heat. Fry garlic and ginger for 1 minute. Add both misos, soy, mirin, sake, and chilli paste. Stir 2 minutes. Add sesame paste and stir until smooth. Set aside.
3. **Build broth:** Bring stock to a boil. Whisk in the tare mixture. Taste — adjust with more miso for saltiness or more mirin for sweetness. Keep warm.
4. **Cook noodles:** Cook ramen noodles per packet instructions. Drain and divide into bowls.
5. **Assemble:** Ladle hot broth over noodles. Top with sliced pork, halved marinated egg, corn, bean sprouts, spring onion, and bamboo shoots. Place a small knob of butter in the centre.
6. **Serve immediately** — ramen waits for no one.

## Chef's Tips

- The butter on top is traditional in Sapporo-style miso ramen — it melts into the broth and adds richness.
- Using both white and red miso creates depth: white adds sweetness, red adds umami.
- Make the broth ahead and reheat — ramen noodles must be freshly cooked.
- Wavy noodles (for miso ramen) grip the thick broth better than straight noodles.

# Katsudon

> A crispy tonkatsu pork cutlet simmered in sweet-savoury dashi broth with egg, served over rice — the beloved Japanese "completion" bowl eaten by students before exams for luck.

## Ingredients

**Tonkatsu:**
- 4 pork loin chops (about 150 g / 5 oz each), 1.5 cm thick
- Salt and pepper
- 50 g (⅓ cup) plain flour
- 2 eggs, beaten
- 100 g (1 cup) panko breadcrumbs
- Neutral oil, for shallow-frying

**Donburi broth:**
- 300 ml (1¼ cups) dashi stock
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 1 large onion, thinly sliced

**Topping:**
- 6 eggs, lightly beaten
- 4 portions steamed Japanese rice
- Spring onion, thinly sliced

## Instructions

1. **Prep pork:** Cut through any fat and sinew around the edges (prevents curling). Season with salt and pepper. Coat each chop in flour (shake off excess), then egg, then panko, pressing firmly.
2. **Fry tonkatsu:** Heat 2 cm of oil in a skillet to 170 °C / 340 °F. Fry cutlets 4 minutes per side until deep golden. Drain on a wire rack. Cut each cutlet into 2 cm strips (keep together).
3. **Make donburi:** In a small sauté pan (22 cm), add one portion of broth: 75 ml dashi, 1 tbsp soy sauce, ¾ tbsp mirin, ½ tbsp sugar, and ¼ of the onion. Simmer 3 minutes until onion softens.
4. **Combine:** Slide one sliced cutlet into the simmering broth. Pour 1½ lightly beaten eggs over the top. Cover and cook 1–2 minutes until just set but still slightly runny.
5. **Serve:** Slide the contents over a rice bowl. Garnish with spring onion. Repeat for remaining portions.

## Chef's Tips

- Rest the panko-coated cutlets on a rack for 5 minutes before frying — the coating adheres better.
- The oil temperature is key: too hot and the outside burns before the pork cooks; use a thermometer.
- Tonkatsu can be fried hours ahead and stored at room temperature — the simmering step reheats it.
- Don't overcook the egg — you want it barely set and creamy, not solid.

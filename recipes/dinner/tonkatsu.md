# Tonkatsu

> Japan's iconic breaded pork cutlet — thick, juicy, encased in a shatteringly crisp panko crust and served with tangy tonkatsu sauce and finely shredded cabbage. Deeply satisfying, deeply simple.

## Ingredients

- 4 pork loin chops, bone-in or boneless (about 180–200 g / 6–7 oz each), 2 cm thick
- Fine salt and black pepper
- 60 g (½ cup) plain flour
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 120 g (1¼ cups) panko breadcrumbs
- Neutral oil, for shallow-frying (about 1.5 cm depth)

**Tonkatsu sauce (or buy Bull-Dog brand):**
- 3 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 2 tbsp ketchup
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp sugar
- ½ tsp Dijon mustard

**To serve:**
- ½ head green cabbage, very finely shredded
- Japanese rice
- Lemon wedges
- Japanese mustard (karashi), optional

## Instructions

1. **Prep pork:** Cut through the fat and connective tissue around the edge at 2 cm intervals — this prevents the cutlet from curling during frying. Season both sides generously with salt and pepper.
2. **Bread:** Set up three shallow bowls: flour, beaten egg, and panko. Coat each chop in flour (shake off excess), then egg (let excess drip), then press firmly into panko on all sides. Rest breaded cutlets on a wire rack for 5 minutes — this helps the coating adhere.
3. **Make sauce:** Mix all sauce ingredients together. Taste and adjust.
4. **Fry:** Heat oil in a deep skillet to 170 °C / 340 °F. Fry 2 cutlets at a time — do not crowd. Cook for 4–5 minutes per side until deep golden brown and internal temperature reaches 63 °C / 145 °F. Drain on a wire rack (never on paper towels — it steams and softens the crust).
5. **Rest and slice:** Rest for 3 minutes. Cut crosswise into 2 cm strips using a sharp knife in one clean motion — don't saw.
6. **Serve:** Place sliced tonkatsu over rice with shredded cabbage alongside. Drizzle tonkatsu sauce over the top. Serve with lemon wedges.

## Chef's Tips

- Panko, not regular breadcrumbs — panko's larger, drier flakes create the signature airy, crunchy crust.
- 170 °C is cooler than most frying temperatures; this allows the thick pork to cook through before the outside burns.
- Resting on a wire rack (not paper towels) preserves the crunch on the underside.
- Tonkatsu is often served with hot Japanese mustard (karashi) on the side — the heat cuts through the richness beautifully.

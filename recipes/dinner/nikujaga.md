# Nikujaga

> Japan's beloved "meat and potatoes" — a sweet, warming stew of beef, potato, and onion in a soy-mirin broth. The quintessential Japanese home-cooked meal.

## Ingredients

- 400 g (14 oz) thinly sliced beef (ribeye or chuck, or use sukiyaki-style beef)
- 600 g (1.3 lbs) waxy potatoes (about 4 medium), peeled and quartered
- 2 medium onions, cut into 8 wedges each
- 2 medium carrots, cut into 2 cm rounds
- 200 g (7 oz) shirataki noodles (konnyaku noodles), drained and rinsed
- 1 tbsp neutral oil

**Broth:**
- 400 ml (1⅔ cups) dashi stock
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp mirin
- 2 tbsp sake
- 1 tbsp sugar

**To serve:**
- Steamed Japanese rice
- Spring onion, sliced

## Instructions

1. **Sear beef:** Heat oil in a large pot over medium-high heat. Add beef slices and cook briefly for 1 minute until just browned. Remove and set aside.
2. **Cook vegetables:** In the same pot, add onions and cook for 2 minutes. Add potatoes and carrots; stir to coat in the beef fat.
3. **Add broth:** Pour in dashi, soy sauce, mirin, sake, and sugar. Stir to combine. Bring to a boil.
4. **Simmer:** Reduce heat to medium-low. Return beef to the pot. Add shirataki noodles. Cook uncovered for 20–25 minutes, gently stirring occasionally, until potatoes are tender and the broth has reduced by about a third.
5. **Taste and adjust** seasoning — add a touch more soy sauce if needed.
6. **Serve** in deep bowls over rice, garnished with spring onion.

## Chef's Tips

- Ask your butcher for "sukiyaki-cut" beef (paper thin) — it absorbs flavour better and stays tender.
- Don't stir too vigorously once the potatoes soften or they break apart.
- Nikujaga tastes better the next day as the flavours deepen — make extra.
- A drop lid (otoshibuta) or a circle of baking paper placed directly on the ingredients promotes even cooking and prevents the potatoes from disintegrating.

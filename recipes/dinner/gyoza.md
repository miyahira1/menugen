# Gyoza

> Crispy-bottomed, juicy-inside Japanese pan-fried dumplings — the pleating takes practice but the reward is extraordinary.

## Ingredients

**Filling:**
- 400 g (14 oz) ground pork (not too lean)
- 200 g (7 oz) napa cabbage (Chinese leaf), very finely chopped
- 3 spring onions, minced
- 3 garlic cloves, grated
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 1 tbsp sake
- 1 tsp sugar
- ½ tsp white pepper

**Assembly:**
- 32–36 round gyoza wrappers (store-bought, about 9 cm / 3.5 in)
- Small bowl of water, for sealing

**To cook:**
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 100 ml (⅓ cup + 1 tbsp) water per batch

**Dipping sauce:**
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp chilli oil

## Instructions

1. **Salt cabbage:** Toss chopped cabbage with ½ tsp salt. Leave 10 minutes, then squeeze out all liquid firmly with your hands — this prevents soggy gyoza.
2. **Make filling:** Combine pork with squeezed cabbage, spring onions, garlic, ginger, soy sauce, sesame oil, sake, sugar, and pepper. Mix vigorously until sticky — this is important for texture.
3. **Fill gyoza:** Place 1 heaped tsp of filling in the centre of a wrapper. Wet the edge with water. Fold in half and pleat the front edge (make 5–6 folds toward the back) to seal. Place pleated-side up. Repeat.
4. **Pan-fry:** Heat oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium-high heat. Add gyoza flat-side down in a single layer (about 12 per batch). Cook 2 minutes until the bottoms are golden.
5. **Steam-fry:** Add 100 ml water carefully (it will spit), cover immediately, and steam for 5 minutes until wrappers are translucent and cooked through.
6. **Crisp:** Remove lid and cook until all water evaporates and bottoms re-crisp, about 2 minutes. Slide onto a plate crispy-side up.
7. **Make dipping sauce** by mixing all ingredients. Serve alongside.

## Chef's Tips

- Squeezing the cabbage is the single most important step — skip it and the filling turns watery.
- Line uncooked gyoza on a tray without touching; freeze them on the tray then transfer to bags for up to 3 months. Cook from frozen, adding 2 extra minutes of steam time.
- Use a lid that traps steam well — this is what makes the top soft while the base crisps.

# Karaage

> Japan's definitive fried chicken — marinated in soy, ginger, and garlic, coated in potato starch for an impossibly light and shatteringly crisp crust.

## Ingredients

- 800 g (1.75 lbs) boneless chicken thighs, cut into 4 cm pieces
- Neutral oil, for frying

**Marinade:**
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp sake
- 1 tbsp mirin
- 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
- 3 garlic cloves, grated
- 1 tsp sesame oil

**Coating:**
- 100 g (¾ cup) potato starch (katakuriko) or cornstarch

**To serve:**
- Japanese mayo (Kewpie)
- Lemon wedges
- Shredded cabbage

## Instructions

1. **Marinate:** Combine all marinade ingredients. Add chicken, toss well, cover, and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes (up to overnight — the longer the better).
2. **Heat oil:** Pour neutral oil into a deep heavy pot to a depth of at least 5 cm (2 in). Heat to 170 °C / 340 °F.
3. **Coat:** Working in batches, toss marinated chicken in potato starch, shaking off all excess. The coating should be thin.
4. **First fry:** Fry in small batches (don't crowd) for 3–4 minutes until pale golden. Remove and drain on a wire rack. Bring oil back to temperature between batches.
5. **Second fry:** Increase oil to 190 °C / 375 °F. Return all chicken and fry for another 1–2 minutes until deeply golden and very crisp. Double-frying is what makes it light rather than greasy.
6. **Serve immediately** with mayo, lemon, and cabbage.

## Chef's Tips

- Potato starch gives a lighter, crisper crust than wheat flour — essential for authentic karaage.
- Thigh meat stays moist; breast meat dries out — always use thighs.
- The second fry at higher heat drives moisture out of the crust; don't skip it.
- Serve within 10 minutes of frying for maximum crunch.

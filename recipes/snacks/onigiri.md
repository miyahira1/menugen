# Onigiri

> Japanese rice balls — portable, satisfying, and infinitely customisable. The ultimate grab-and-go snack that every Japanese household knows how to make.

## Ingredients

**Rice:**
- 400 g (2 cups) Japanese short-grain rice
- 480 ml (2 cups) cold water
- 1 tsp fine salt

**Filling (choose 1–2):**
- Tuna mayo: 1 tin tuna drained + 3 tbsp Japanese mayo + pinch of salt
- Salmon: 150 g cooked or tinned salmon, flaked
- Umeboshi: 4 pickled plums (ume), pitted and roughly chopped

**To finish:**
- 4 sheets nori (seaweed), each cut into thirds
- Salt, for hands
- Sesame seeds, optional (for outside)

## Instructions

1. **Cook rice:** Rinse rice until water runs clear (about 5 rinses). Add to pot with 480 ml cold water. Bring to boil over high heat, then reduce to the lowest simmer, cover tightly, and cook 12 minutes. Remove from heat; rest covered for 10 minutes. Do not lift the lid.
2. **Season rice:** Season lightly with salt and fold gently. Spread on a tray to cool until just handleable (not cold).
3. **Prepare filling:** Mix tuna with mayo; season with salt. Or simply use the other fillings as-is.
4. **Shape:** Wet your hands with cold water and rub with a little salt. Take about ½ cup (100 g) of rice. Flatten in your palm, add 1 tbsp of filling in the centre, then fold rice around it. Press firmly into a triangle shape, rotating and pressing all three sides. The rice should hold together firmly.
5. **Wrap:** Press a strip of nori around the base of each onigiri. Serve immediately or wrap in cling film to pack.

## Chef's Tips

- Warm hands melt the rice — wet cold hands are key to a clean shape.
- For crispy onigiri (yaki onigiri), brush with soy sauce and grill or pan-fry until golden.
- Japanese mayo (Kewpie) is noticeably richer than regular — worth seeking out.
- Onigiri keep at room temperature for 4–6 hours; do not refrigerate (the rice hardens).

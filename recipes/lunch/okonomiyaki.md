# Okonomiyaki

> Osaka's beloved savoury pancake — a thick, hearty mix of cabbage, egg, and pork, topped with a sweet-savoury sauce and Japanese mayo. "Okonomi" means "as you like it" — endlessly customisable.

## Ingredients

**Batter:**
- 200 g (1½ cups) plain flour
- ½ tsp baking powder
- 240 ml (1 cup) dashi stock (or water)
- 4 large eggs
- ½ tsp fine salt

**Filling:**
- ½ head green cabbage (~600 g / 1.3 lbs), finely shredded
- 200 g (7 oz) pork belly, thinly sliced (or bacon)
- 4 spring onions, thinly sliced
- 2 tbsp neutral oil

**Toppings:**
- 4 tbsp okonomiyaki sauce (or: 2 tbsp Worcestershire + 2 tbsp ketchup + 1 tbsp soy + 1 tsp sugar)
- 4 tbsp Japanese mayo (Kewpie)
- Aonori (dried seaweed flakes)
- Katsuobushi (dried bonito flakes)
- Beni shoga (pickled ginger)

## Instructions

1. **Make batter:** Whisk flour, baking powder, dashi, eggs, and salt until smooth. Rest for 5 minutes.
2. **Combine:** Fold shredded cabbage and spring onions into the batter. The mixture should be mostly cabbage with just enough batter to bind — don't worry if it looks dry; cabbage releases moisture.
3. **Cook (one pancake at a time):** Heat ½ tbsp oil in a 24 cm non-stick skillet over medium heat. Pour in ¼ of the batter mixture (about 2 cups), shaping into a round about 2 cm thick. Lay 2–3 pork slices on top.
4. **First side:** Cook for 5–6 minutes until the underside is deep golden and the edges look set.
5. **Flip:** Slide onto a plate, put the pan upside down on top, and flip together — or use two spatulas confidently. Cook the pork side for 5 minutes.
6. **Finish:** Flip back so pork faces up. Cook another 2 minutes. The pancake should feel firm when pressed.
7. **Sauce and toppings:** Spread okonomiyaki sauce, then drizzle mayo in a zigzag pattern. Top with aonori, katsuobushi, and beni shoga. Serve immediately.

## Chef's Tips

- Resting the batter lets the gluten relax — the pancake holds together better.
- Don't press the pancake down while cooking; you want a light, airy interior.
- Yakisoba noodles mixed into the batter is the "modern-yaki" style and makes it more substantial.
- The katsuobushi "dances" from the heat of the pancake — this is normal and delightful.

# Yakisoba

> Japan's quintessential stir-fried noodle dish — chewy wheat noodles tossed with pork, cabbage, and tangy-sweet Worcestershire-based sauce. Festival food made at home in 30 minutes.

## Ingredients

- 4 portions fresh yakisoba noodles (or ramen noodles, ~600 g total)
- 300 g (10 oz) pork belly or shoulder, thinly sliced
- ¼ head green cabbage, roughly chopped
- 1 medium carrot, julienned
- 1 onion, thinly sliced
- 2 spring onions, sliced
- 2 tbsp neutral oil

**Yakisoba sauce:**
- 3 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 2 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp ketchup
- 1 tsp sugar

**Toppings:**
- Aonori (dried green seaweed flakes)
- Beni shoga (red pickled ginger)
- Katsuobushi (dried bonito flakes), optional
- Japanese mayo, optional

## Instructions

1. **Mix sauce:** Combine Worcestershire, oyster sauce, soy sauce, ketchup, and sugar in a bowl. Set aside.
2. **Loosen noodles:** If using packaged fresh noodles, loosen them under warm running water or according to packet instructions.
3. **Stir-fry pork:** Heat 1 tbsp oil in a large wok or skillet over high heat. Add pork and cook for 3 minutes until browned. Season lightly with salt. Remove and set aside.
4. **Cook vegetables:** Add remaining oil. Add onion and carrot; stir-fry 2 minutes. Add cabbage; stir-fry 2 more minutes until wilted but still with some bite.
5. **Add noodles:** Add noodles to the wok. Toss vigorously over high heat for 2 minutes, separating any clumps.
6. **Sauce:** Return pork. Pour sauce over everything. Toss and stir-fry 1–2 minutes until every strand is coated and glossy.
7. **Serve:** Plate and top with aonori, beni shoga, and katsuobushi (which will dance from the heat). Add mayo if desired.

## Chef's Tips

- High heat and fast movement are essential — don't let anything steam or stew.
- Beni shoga (pickled red ginger) is not optional for authenticity; the acidic contrast cuts through the rich sauce.
- Yakisoba sauce can be bought pre-made at Japanese grocery stores — worth having on hand.
- Add a fried egg on top (hanjuku style — runny yolk) for a satisfying complete meal.
